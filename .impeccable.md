## Design Context

### Users

Punctra's browser interface is for web developers embedding LAS/LAZ point-cloud
rendering in their own applications. They need to confirm browser and WebGPU
capabilities, understand ownership and resource limits, exercise the renderer,
and diagnose unsupported states without adopting a complete editor.

### Brand Personality

Restrained, dependable, and engineering-led. The interface should feel like an
older professional engineering workstation: direct, durable, and factual rather
than fashionable or promotional. It should create confidence through explicit
state and precise language.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a quiet grey palette, compact information density, square or lightly rounded
geometry, strong alignment, and utilitarian typography. Prefer the character of
technical instruments, printed equipment labels, and established desktop tools.
Do not use glossy effects, decorative gradients, neon accents, oversized hero
copy, playful illustration, or conspicuous animation. The interface should not
look fancy.

### Design Principles

1. Put renderer state, capability facts, limits, and recovery actions ahead of
   decoration.
2. Use restrained grey surfaces with WCAG AA contrast and never rely on color
   alone for status.
3. Keep controls familiar, compact, keyboard accessible, and usable at narrow
   browser widths.
4. Use little or no motion; honor `prefers-reduced-motion` and avoid continuous
   decorative animation.
5. Clearly separate CPU-authoritative facts from provisional GPU display and
   picking evidence.
